01 / The problem

Early-childhood educators faced different kinds of professional-development records with inconsistent ways of finding and reading them.

02 / Designed for

Early-childhood educators reviewing training, qualifications, coaching, and other professional-development evidence.

03 / My role

UI/UX designer for the professional-development record and its responsive, interactive patterns.

How evidence became a design decision

01 — RESEARCH / EVIDENCE

What I examined

Compared the record types, table fields, overlays, and actions required across the existing experience.

02 — INSIGHT

What I learned

The information could vary without forcing educators to learn a new interaction model for every tab.

03 — DESIGN DECISION

What I changed

Use one searchable, sortable table pattern across the record tabs, plus consistent overlays and feedback states.

04 — OUTCOME

What resulted

Responsive design deliverables and interaction states were prepared; usage and task-success improvements were not measured.

Why this decision?Learning a familiar pattern once lowers the effort of navigating the next record type.

SSituation

01 · the tension

One record, eight kinds of proof

The Professional Development Record is the official IdahoSTARS account of every training, credential, course and coaching session an early childhood educator completes. Growth shows up in very different forms: approved training hours, first aid and CPR certifications, diplomas and college credit, scholarships and payments, technical assistance hours, coaching sessions, and group cohorts.

Eight kinds of proof, one record. The problem is not storing them — it is making them readable as one person's history.

TTask & action

02 · the table

Once you can read one, you can read them all

Each record type gets its own tab, and every tab uses the same searchable, paginated table. Training lists course ID, name, date, domain and CDA subject area; every column sorts, and the Type filter narrows the table to one kind of training.

Holding the pattern steady across nine tabs is the whole move. A new record type costs the user nothing to learn, because it behaves exactly like the last one.

AAction & details

03 · the overlays

Designing the moments in between

The work that decides whether a redesign survives contact with a build is not the screens — it is the overlays. Building a report: pick a date range, the record types to include and a facility, then view it on screen or download it. Reading it: each selected type becomes its own section with a running total, such as IdahoSTARS-approved hours or credit hours.

A coaching session opens to its notes and action plan — the goal, the steps to reach it with dates, and what the coach will do. Adding evidence is one upload flow for both documents and video: the upload button relabels itself to match the file type, and a permission checkbox appears only for video.

Each one was handed off annotated with the action, the result and the edge cases, so developers built behavior and not just pixels.

04 · microinteractions

The small responses that say it worked

Seven interactions, drawn state by state as they were given to developers. Sorting runs ascending then descending on any column. The Type filter narrows a table to one kind of record. Only the report types you tick appear in the report. Choosing Video relabels the upload button and adds the permission check — and if you skip it, the error says exactly how to fix it. Deleting a document always asks first.

They are small, and they are the difference between a table a person trusts and one they double-check somewhere else.

05 · smaller

Down to tablet and phone

The record and its descriptions were designed for tablet and phone widths as well as desktop. A record an educator can only reach at a desk is a record that gets updated late, or not at all.

RResult & reflection

06 · what next

What I cannot claim

No adoption numbers, no task times, no error rates. The redesign was delivered as screens, overlays and interaction annotations, and the outcome data is not mine to publish.

What I would measure: how often an educator opens a record and leaves without finding what they came for, how many uploads fail the permission step on first try, and whether the report gets built on screen or downloaded — because that last one says whether the on-screen report is worth keeping.
